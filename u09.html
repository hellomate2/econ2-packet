Firms, Costs & Profit

Classes 8–9 · Chapters 6–7 Midterm + Final

The supply side, derived properly. Firms maximize economic profit = total revenue − total cost, where total cost means the opportunity cost of all inputs.

Under perfect competition each firm is small enough that its own output does not move the market price. So while market demand slopes down, the demand curve an individual firm faces is horizontal at the market price — and therefore marginal revenue is flat and equal to P.

π = P·q − C(q) ⟹ ∂π/∂q = P − MC = 0 ⟹ P = MCGenerally MR = MC. For a competitive firm MR = P, so the rule collapses to P = MC.

Costs, carefully

  • Fixed cost Cf: does not vary with output — drilling the well, the facility, the equipment.
  • Variable cost Cv(q): varies with output — the labor and materials used up.
  • Marginal cost MC = C′(q) = Cv′(q). Fixed costs drop out, since they do not change when you make one more.
  • Average total cost ATC = C(q)/q = Cf/q + Cv(q)/q.

ATC starts high because fixed costs are spread over few units, falls, then rises. MC crosses ATC at exactly the minimum of ATC — because when MC is below ATC it drags the average down, and when MC is above it pushes the average up.

And the profit formula you actually use on exams:

Profit = (P − ATC) × qProfits are positive, zero or negative depending on whether the price is above, at, or below average total cost at the chosen quantity.

Entry, exit and the long run

Profits are signals. Positive profits attract entry, which shifts market supply out and drives the price down. Negative profits cause exit, which shifts supply in and drives the price up. Zero profit is the resting point — no force pushing either way. This is Adam Smith's invisible hand, and it explains why economists dislike barriers to entry.

If all firms are identical, the long-run industry supply curve is perfectly elastic — a horizontal line at minimum ATC, where P = MC = ATC and profit is zero for everyone.

If firms differ in productivity — better and worse oil wells — the long-run supply curve slopes up. Every producing firm still sets P = MC, the marginal (least productive) firm earns zero profit, and the more productive firms earn positive profits. Those profits are really returns to their better wells.

The moves
  1. Find quantity from MR = MC. For a competitive firm that is P = MC.
  2. Compare P to ATC at that quantity to sign the profit.
  3. Draw profit as a rectangle, never a triangle. Height (P − ATC), width q.
  4. For the long run, ask whether entry is free. Free entry ⇒ zero profit for the marginal firm.
Traps
  • Confusing producer surplus with profit. Producer surplus ignores fixed costs; profit subtracts them.
  • Letting fixed costs into marginal cost. They are not there.
  • Shutting down because profit is negative in the short run. If price covers variable cost, keep producing.
  • Saying zero profit means the firm is doing badly. Economic profit is zero when all opportunity costs, including the owner's, are covered.
Drills · Unit 9
conceptWhy is the demand curve facing a single competitive firm horizontal when market demand slopes down?
Answer

Because the firm is small relative to the market. Its own output choice has no perceptible effect on the market price, so it can sell as much as it wants at the going price and nothing at all above it. A North Dakota oil rig takes the world oil price as given.

The consequence: marginal revenue is flat and equal to P, so profit maximization reduces from MR = MC to P = MC.

examOnce a farmer owns a chicken farm, what determines the marginal cost of an egg, and is it high or low? What does that imply about the supply curve?
Answer

Marginal cost is just the cost of keeping one more laying hen — feed, water, henhouse space, some fencing or a guard dog. A hen lays about one egg a day. These costs are low, so the marginal cost of an egg is low.

The supply curve therefore slopes upward but sits low: marginal cost still rises with production, as it does for most physical goods, but starts from a low base. Note the farm itself is a fixed cost and does not enter marginal cost at all.

conceptWhy does the MC curve always cross the ATC curve at ATC's minimum?
Answer

Pure arithmetic of averages. When MC < ATC, producing one more unit drags the average down. When MC > ATC, it pulls the average up. The average therefore stops falling and starts rising exactly where MC passes through it — its minimum.

With calculus: setting ∂(C(q)/q)/∂q = 0 gives C′(q) = C(q)/q, which is MC = ATC.

graphDraw a competitive firm making positive economic profit. Then show what happens to the market and the firm in the long run.
Answer

Short run: flat MR at P₁ above the ATC curve. Quantity q₁ at MC = MR. Profit is the rectangle (P₁ − ATC₁) × q₁.

Long run: those profits attract entry. Market supply shifts out, the market price falls, and the firm's flat MR line slides down. Entry stops only when P equals minimum ATC, at which point P = MC = ATC and economic profit is zero.

With identical firms the long-run industry supply curve is a horizontal line at that minimum ATC.

conceptIf long-run profit is zero for the marginal firm, why does anyone stay in business?
Answer

Because economic profit is not accounting profit. Total cost is the opportunity cost of all inputs, including the owner's time and the return their capital could earn elsewhere. Zero economic profit means every input — owner included — is earning exactly what it could earn in its next best use.

Also, only the marginal firm earns zero. When firms differ in productivity, the better ones earn genuine positive profits, which are really returns to their superior wells, land or technology.
