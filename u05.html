Price Controls

Classes 5–6 · Chapter 6 Midterm
If you remember one thing

A ceiling binds only below equilibrium, and then quantity traded is the short side, QS, read off supply. Total surplus falls, but consumer surplus is ambiguous.

Definitions

Price ceiling
Legal maximum, binding only if set below equilibrium. Berkeley imposed rent control until 1998; since 1999 "stabilization" applies only after a lease starts.
Price floor
Legal minimum, binding only if set above equilibrium; quantity traded is then QD.
Shortage
Quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied — the horizontal gap QD − QS at the controlled price.
Rationing
Price cannot clear the market, so the good goes by queuing, government rationing (COVID vaccines, 2021) or connections.

Two losses, not one

LossWhat it is
Deadweight lossThe triangle between supply and demand from QS to Q₁ — trades with MB > MC that never happen
MisallocationThe diagram assumes units go to the highest-value buyers; in a shortage nothing enforces that. An elderly tenant holds a rent-controlled apartment a wealthier student would pay far more for and cannot get

Naming misallocation separately from deadweight loss earns real credit.

The honest case for rent control

  1. It genuinely benefits consumers who get a unit.
  2. It insures tenants against price swings from demand and supply shocks — the static model does not price that.
  3. The alternative is subsidized public housing: government controls price and quantity, widely used worldwide, not subject to the same shortage mechanism.

Shortages without price controls

Prices are sticky, so queues appear anyway: Saturday-night restaurants, street parking on game days, and Uber surge pricing that customers punish firms for using. The labor market queues too — in recessions the unemployed cannot instantly bid wages down, and in booms employers cannot bid wages up for new hires only.

Rules that earn points

  1. State whether the control binds. A ceiling above equilibrium does nothing.
  2. Mark quantity traded at the short side — QS under a ceiling, QD under a floor.
  3. Label the shortage as a horizontal gap, not a vertical one.
  4. Say "deadweight loss plus misallocation among consumers."
Traps
  • Reading quantity off the demand curve under a ceiling. Nothing forces suppliers past QS.
  • Asserting consumer surplus falls. It can rise — the transfer from producers may exceed the triangle lost.
  • Drawing a ceiling above the equilibrium price and then finding a shortage.
Unit 5 quiz4 MCQ · 2 FRQ · ~12 min
MCQ 12 pts

Under a binding price ceiling, the quantity actually traded is read off:

  • AThe demand curve at the ceiling price
  • BThe supply curve at the ceiling price
  • CThe demand curve at the equilibrium price
  • DWhichever of QD and QS is larger
Answer

B — supply, at the ceiling price.

Trade happens on the short side. Nothing forces producers to make more than QS at a price they did not choose.

A is the trap: QD is what buyers want at that price. Wanting it is why there is a shortage, not why it gets produced.

MCQ 22 pts

A binding price ceiling is imposed. Consumer surplus:

  • AAlways rises, since consumers pay less
  • BAlways falls, since less is traded
  • CCan rise or fall, depending on the elasticities
  • DIs unchanged; only producer surplus moves
Answer

C — ambiguous.

Consumers gain a rectangle transferred from producers on the units still traded, but lose a triangle on units no longer traded. Which dominates depends on the elasticities. Total surplus, unlike consumer surplus, unambiguously falls.

A is the trap: it counts the transfer and forgets that many buyers now get nothing at all.

MCQ 32 pts

A city sets a rent ceiling above the equilibrium rent. The result is:

  • AA shortage of apartments
  • BA surplus of apartments
  • CNo effect — the control does not bind
  • DRents rise to the ceiling
Answer

C — no effect.

A ceiling is a legal maximum. If the market already clears below it, the constraint never binds and the equilibrium is undisturbed.

D is the trap: a ceiling is a cap, not a target. Nothing pulls the price up to it.

MCQ 42 pts

The true welfare cost of a binding price ceiling is larger than the deadweight loss triangle because:

  • AProducers exit the market entirely
  • BThe units still traded may not go to the buyers who value them most
  • CThe government spends money enforcing the control
  • DDemand becomes more elastic under the control
Answer

B — misallocation among consumers.

The triangle is computed assuming the highest-value buyers are served first. With a shortage, allocation happens by queuing, luck or connections instead, and that lost value sits on top of the triangle.

C is the trap: enforcement costs are real but are not the welfare loss the diagram is hiding.

FRQ 16 pts

Draw a binding price ceiling. Label the shortage, the new consumer and producer surplus, and the deadweight loss. Does consumer surplus rise or fall?

One diagram plus three sentences. Axes P and Q; label P₁, Q₁, PC, QS, QD, and shade every region you name.
Model answer

Horizontal line at PC below P₁. Quantity traded is QS, read off supply. The shortage is the horizontal gap QD − QS at that price.

Consumer surplus is the area under demand and above PC out to QS; producer surplus is the small triangle under PC and above supply; the deadweight loss is the triangle between supply and demand from QS to Q₁.

Consumer surplus is ambiguous. Consumers gain a rectangle transferred from producers but lose a triangle from units no longer traded, and which dominates depends on the elasticities. Total surplus unambiguously falls.

What earns the points
  • Ceiling drawn below P₁, and quantity taken from supply.
  • Shortage marked as the horizontal gap at PC.
  • All three regions shaded and named.
  • Consumer surplus called ambiguous, with the transfer-versus-triangle reason.
  • Total surplus stated as unambiguously lower.
FRQ 24 pts

Give two arguments for rent control that the simple diagram leaves out, and name the main policy alternative.

Four sentences, no diagram. Two arguments, then the alternative and why it works differently.
Model answer

First, it genuinely benefits tenants who secure a unit — a real distributional gain the efficiency calculation ignores. Second, it provides insurance against price volatility caused by demand and supply shocks, which is valuable to households and absent from the static model.

The main alternative is subsidized public housing, where the government sets both price and quantity, so supply does not contract in response to the controlled price. It is used widely around the world.

What earns the points
  • Two distinct arguments — distributional gain and insurance, not the same point twice.
  • Saying explicitly that the static diagram prices neither one.
  • Naming subsidized public housing.
  • Explaining why it differs: government controls quantity as well as price, so the shortage mechanism does not apply.
