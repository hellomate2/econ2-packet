The Graph Vault

Blank-paper recall · all 36 diagrams

Both past exams award 4 points a time for "draw and label." That is the densest scoring opportunity in the course, and it is pure recall.

Work this list with a blank sheet. For each one, draw it, label it, then scroll up to the unit and check. A diagram scores when it has: both axes named, every curve named, the equilibrium marked and its coordinates stated where known, and every shaded region named. An unlabelled correct drawing earns very little.

Recall drills · draw each from memory
micro coreDraw from blank: (1) bowed PPC with efficient, unattainable and recession points; (2) supply and demand with a shortage and a surplus; (3) consumer and producer surplus; (4) deadweight loss from under- and over-production.
Checklist
  • PPC: axes are two goods. Point on the curve, point beyond it, point inside it. Slope = minus the opportunity cost of the horizontal good.
  • S&D: axes P and Q. Shortage and surplus drawn as horizontal gaps at off-equilibrium prices.
  • Surplus: CS above price under demand; PS below price above supply. Label demand as D, MB and supply as S, MC.
  • DWL: triangle between the curves, point at the efficient quantity, opening toward the actual quantity. Works in both directions.
interventionsDraw from blank: (5) binding price ceiling with shortage and DWL; (6) tax wedge with revenue and DWL; (7) tax incidence under elastic vs inelastic demand; (8) subsidy with government cost and DWL.
Checklist
  • Ceiling: horizontal line below P₁. Quantity traded reads off supply, not demand. Shortage is the horizontal gap.
  • Tax: vertical wedge of height = tax. Three prices marked — buyers pay, sellers keep, original. Revenue is a rectangle; DWL is the triangle to its right.
  • Incidence: two panels. Steep demand ⇒ consumer price rises nearly the full tax, small DWL. Flat demand ⇒ producers absorb it, big DWL.
  • Subsidy: quantity past Q₁. Government rectangle exceeds the surplus gained; the difference is the triangle.
firmsDraw from blank: (9) competitive firm at MR = MC; (10) MC crossing ATC at its minimum with the profit rectangle; (11) long-run industry supply; (12) monopoly with MR, DWL and the competitive benchmark.
Checklist
  • Competitive firm: MR flat at P. q where MC = MR. Lowercase letters for the firm, uppercase for the market.
  • ATC: U-shaped, MC cutting it exactly at the bottom. Profit is a rectangle, height (P − ATC), width q.
  • LR supply: horizontal at min ATC with identical firms; upward sloping when firms differ in productivity.
  • Monopoly: MR shares demand's intercept and is twice as steep. Q from MR = MC, then P read up on demand. Competitive Q where MC meets D. DWL between them.
households & externalitiesDraw from blank: (13) budget constraint with an income shift and a price pivot; (14) total vs marginal utility; (15) negative externality with the corrective tax; (16) positive externality with the corrective subsidy.
Checklist
  • Budget line: intercepts Income/P on each axis, slope −Px/Py. Income shifts parallel; a price change pivots around the other good's intercept.
  • Utility: total increasing and concave; marginal falling beneath it.
  • Negative externality: SMC above PMC. Q* < Q₁. Tax equal to external MC at Q* shifts supply onto SMC.
  • Positive externality: SMB above PMB. Q* > Q₁. Subsidy equal to external MB shifts demand onto SMB.
factors & tradeDraw from blank: (17) labor market with MRPL; (18) skill-biased technological change in two panels; (19) EITC; (20) investment demand against r; (21) PPC with CPC; (22) export and import goods with a world price; (23) tariff welfare with both DWL triangles.
Checklist
  • Labor: axes W and L. Demand labelled MRPL = MPL × MR — write the formula on the curve.
  • Skill bias: two panels, demand out only in the high-skill panel.
  • EITC: labor supply shifts out; market wage falls; workers get W₂ + credit.
  • Investment: axes I and r, downward sloping. Tax credit shifts out, pessimism shifts in.
  • CPC: straight line, slope = −terms of trade, tangent to the PPC, lying outside it.
  • Tariff: two horizontal price lines. Revenue over remaining imports. Two DWL triangles, d and f.
macroDraw from blank: (24) Keynesian Cross with equilibrium; (25) a PAE shift showing the multiplier; (26) fiscal offset; (27) the Fed's reaction function; (28) return to potential; (29) the market for dollars; (30) S + NKI = I.
Checklist
  • Keynesian Cross: axes Y and PAE. 45° line labelled Y = PAE. PAE line with intercept = autonomous spending, slope = mpc. Slope must be visibly less than 45°.
  • Multiplier: vertical drop = the shock; horizontal move in Y is larger. Annotate both.
  • Reaction function: axes π and r, upward sloping, crossing a horizontal r* line at πTARGET. Shifting it up lowers long-run inflation.
  • Return to potential: PAE shifting until it crosses the 45° line exactly at Y*.
  • FX: axes are quantity of dollars and price of dollars in foreign currency. Demand = foreigners buying US; supply = Americans buying abroad.
  • S + NKI = I: axes r* and quantity. Investment downward, S + NKI upward.
