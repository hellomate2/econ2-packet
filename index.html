How to use this

The method

Rereading is the least effective thing you can do. Every unit ends in a quiz. Take it closed-book — write the answer out, then reveal. Tick the box only if you got the substance.

Each unit, in order

  1. Read the highlighted box. That is the one idea the unit is built around.
  2. Cover the graph and redraw it on blank paper — axes, curves, equilibrium, shaded areas. Then compare.
  3. Skim the rules. These are the sentence patterns that actually score.
  4. Take the quiz cold. 4 multiple choice, 2 free response, written the way Edwards writes them.

Edwards writes on the exam: “Confine your answers to the space provided. Don’t fill the space with words. Less is more.” He means it. Three tight sentences and a correct diagram beat a paragraph of hedging.

The four sentences that carry the course

  1. Demand slopes down because marginal benefit falls. Every demand curve here is a willingness-to-pay curve.
  2. Supply slopes up because marginal cost rises. Every supply curve here is a marginal cost curve.
  3. Efficiency is MB = MC. Any wedge — tax, monopoly, externality, tariff, price control — creates deadweight loss.
  4. Optimizers set marginal benefit equal to marginal cost. Households: mu/P equal across goods. Firms: MR = MC. Hiring: MRPL = W. Machines: PV of MRPK = price.
