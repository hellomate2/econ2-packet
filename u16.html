Trade Policy & Tariffs

Class 16 · Chapter 15 Final

Now put trade into the supply-and-demand diagram you already know. Draw the domestic market, then draw the world price as a horizontal line — the country is a price taker on world markets, so world supply and demand at that price are perfectly elastic.

  • Pworld above the no-trade domestic price ⇒ the country exports. Domestic quantity supplied exceeds domestic quantity demanded, and the horizontal gap is exports.
  • Pworld below the domestic price ⇒ the country imports. Domestic quantity demanded exceeds domestic quantity supplied, and the gap is imports.

Trade moves jobs rather than destroying them

Run it through MRPL = MPL × MR. In an export industry the world price is above the old domestic price, so MR rises and labor demand shifts up — wages and employment rise. In an import-competing industry the world price is below the old domestic price, so MR falls and labor demand shifts down — wages and employment fall.

Trade rearranges jobs rather than raising or lowering total employment. But the rearrangement can be brutal for workers who lack the skills to move to where the new jobs are.

The China Shock

Before 2001, the consensus was that trade did not much jar labor markets: manufacturing employment had been declining since roughly the Korean War, earlier trade liberalizations produced no big dislocations, and US wage inequality had emerged before any large trade changes. Theory said displaced workers would move, and impacts would be diffuse.

China's WTO accession in late 2001 broke that. Autor, Dorn and Hanson showed the shock hit geographic agglomerations — commuting zones concentrated in exposed industries — and those labor markets did not recover. Manufacturing and total employment fell persistently; older workers moved onto disability and, in some accounts, opioids.

The paired-towns comparison makes it concrete. North Hickory, North Carolina was a furniture town with a high manufacturing employment share and few college graduates; Raleigh-Cary had similar exposure but was larger, more industrially diversified and more educated. Raleigh-Cary ended up far better off — the damage was about concentration and adjustment capacity, not exposure alone.

Tariffs

Methods of protection are tariffs (a tax on imports), quotas (a quantity limit), and subsidies for domestic production. A tariff raises the domestic price to Pworld + tariff, which pulls domestic production up and domestic consumption down, squeezing imports from both sides.

Employment effects of a tariff: it likely raises employment in the protected industry, but probably not total employment. Retaliation reduces foreign demand for your exports; the currency may appreciate, which hurts exporters (Unit 24); and workers in other industries get hit by higher input costs.

Legitimate arguments for protection: national security, supply chain disruption, diversification, infant industries and dynamic comparative advantage, protecting specific workers, and positive domestic externalities from domestic production. Edwards adds a dry final item to the list: capriciousness and punishment.

Trade policy is also not the only thing determining trade. Shipping costs, logistics and communication matter — the container ship of the 1950s transformed trade by cutting out the stevedores who controlled loading.

The moves
  1. Draw domestic S and D, then the horizontal world price line. Above autarky ⇒ exports; below ⇒ imports.
  2. Mark QS and QD at the world price and label the horizontal gap as exports or imports.
  3. For a tariff, add a second horizontal line at Pworld + tariff and mark the new QS and QD.
  4. Revenue is the rectangle over the remaining imports. There are two deadweight loss triangles, one on each side.
Traps
  • Drawing only one deadweight loss triangle for a tariff. There are two: d and f.
  • Putting tariff revenue over total imports. It is only the imports that still occur.
  • Claiming tariffs raise total employment. They shift it, and retaliation and currency appreciation push back.
Drills · Unit 16
examAdapt a domestic supply and demand diagram to show about half of quantity supplied being exported. Where must the world price sit, and what shows exports?
Answer

The world price Pw must be above the price that would equate domestic supply and demand — otherwise producers would sell at home instead.

Draw Pw as a horizontal line above the autarky equilibrium. At that price, domestic quantity supplied QS is large and domestic quantity demanded QD is small. The horizontal gap between them is exports. For "about half," place Pw so that gap is roughly half of QS.

examSoybeans are planted in spring and harvested in fall. If farmers planted before a trade war halted Chinese purchases, and soybeans can't be stored or destroyed, how far must the price fall?
Answer

Supply is now perfectly inelastic — a vertical line at the already-planted quantity QSUS. With world demand gone, the entire crop must clear in the domestic market alone.

The price falls all the way down to the domestic demand curve read at QSUS — a really big reduction, far below the old world price. That is the whole story of the soybean bailouts: $23 billion in the first Trump term, with $10–12 billion more planned in 2025.

conceptWhy did the China Shock hurt so much more than earlier trade liberalizations?
Answer

Because it hit geographically concentrated labor markets. Earlier episodes spread job losses thinly across industries and places, so workers could be absorbed. China's 2001 WTO accession hit commuting zones built around single exposed industries — furniture in the Carolinas being the standard example.

Autor, Dorn and Hanson found those places did not recover: manufacturing and total employment fell persistently, and older workers moved onto disability rather than to new jobs.

The paired-town evidence isolates the mechanism. North Hickory, NC — furniture-dependent, few college graduates — fared far worse than Raleigh-Cary, which had similar exposure but was diversified and educated. Concentration and adjustment capacity, not exposure, did the damage.

conceptName four defensible arguments for protection.
Answer
  • National security — you do not want to import your weapons or your food from a rival.
  • Supply chain disruption and the case for diversification, which COVID made vivid.
  • Infant industry / dynamic comparative advantage — an industry that would be competitive at scale may need protection to get there.
  • Positive domestic externalities from domestic production, such as technology spillovers or a skilled workforce.

"Jobs for particular workers" is also on the list, though it is a distributional argument rather than an efficiency one.
