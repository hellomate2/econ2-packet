GDP & Measurement

Class 17 · Chapter 17 Final

GDP is the market value of final goods and services newly produced in a country during a period. Every word in that definition is load-bearing: final (not intermediate, to avoid double counting), newly produced (not resales), in a country, in a period.

The identity worth internalizing: total production = total income = total spending. Your spending is someone's income. That is why the same number can be measured three ways:

  • Expenditure: Y = C + I + G + NX. This is the one you will use.
  • Production (value added): follow goods through each stage.
  • Income: labor income plus capital income.

Anchor numbers: US GDP is about $30 trillion for 340 million people, so GDP per person is roughly $88,000. Shares of GDP: C is 65–70%, I is 15–20%, G is 15–20%, NX is about −3%.

Real versus nominal

Nominal means measured in current dollars. Real means adjusted for price changes — measured in goods and services rather than dollars.

Nominal GDP2022 = Σ Pi,2022 · Qi,2022
Real GDP2022 (2012 base) = Σ Pi,2012 · Qi,2022Real GDP answers: what would this year's output have been worth at base-year prices? Fix prices, let quantities move.

Growth rate of real GDP is the percentage change from one year to the next: (Xt − Xt−1)/Xt−1 × 100.

Comparing across countries

Currency units break down across space as well as time. China's 2024 nominal GDP was ¥134,908 billion and the US's was $29,298 billion — not directly comparable. Converting at market exchange rates also misleads, because non-traded goods are cheaper in poorer countries. So the IMF uses purchasing power parity "international dollars," which put China at about $38.2 trillion and the US at $29.3 trillion for 2024. On a PPP basis China's economy is larger; at market exchange rates it is not. Knowing why those two statements coexist is the point.

The long run versus the short run

Potential output Y* is what the economy produces using its resources at normal rates. Edwards notes a better name would be "normal output" — it is not a ceiling, since Y can exceed it temporarily. Decompose it:

Y*/POP = (N*/POP) × (Y*/N*)Normal output per person = normal employment-to-population ratio × normal output per worker (average labor productivity). And productivity depends on capital per worker K*/N* and technology T.

The three determinants are therefore labor, capital and technology, where capital includes physical capital, infrastructure and human capital, and technology means production techniques, management techniques, economic institutions and culture.

The moves
  1. Write Y = C + I + G + NX before answering anything about GDP composition.
  2. If prices are involved, say explicitly whether you are working in real or nominal terms.
  3. For cross-country comparisons, name PPP and say why market exchange rates mislead.
  4. For long-run questions, decompose into employment ratio, capital per worker, and technology.
Traps
  • Counting intermediate goods or resales in GDP.
  • Using current-year prices for real GDP. Real GDP holds prices fixed at the base year.
  • Treating potential output as a hard maximum. Y can exceed Y* in a boom.
Drills · Unit 17
conceptWhy is China's economy bigger than the US's on one measure and smaller on another?
Answer

Because the two measures convert currencies differently. At market exchange rates, US nominal GDP (about $29.3 trillion in 2024) exceeds China's converted yuan figure.

At purchasing power parity, which prices output in "international dollars" reflecting what money actually buys locally, China comes out at roughly $38.2 trillion against the US at $29.3 trillion.

The reason PPP is higher for China: non-traded goods and services are much cheaper there, so a dollar converted at market rates buys more real output than the exchange rate suggests. PPP is the better measure for comparing living standards and real output; market rates are better for comparing purchasing power on world markets.

conceptDecompose potential output per person and name what determines each piece.
Answer
Y*/POP = (N*/POP) × (Y*/N*)
  • N*/POP, the normal employment-to-population ratio — demographics and labor force participation.
  • Y*/N*, normal average labor productivity — which depends on capital per worker K*/N* (physical capital, infrastructure, human capital) and on technology T.

Technology here is broad: production techniques, management techniques, economic institutions, and culture — everything affecting output per worker given capital per worker.
