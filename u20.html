The Keynesian Cross

Class 19 · Chapter 24 Final — high value

The single highest-value diagram on the final. The past exam gave it a 24-point question. Learn to produce it numerically and graphically.

Why demand determines output in the short run: prices do not adjust quickly or completely, because of limited information, long-term contracts, menu costs, consumer dislike of "unfair" price changes and worker resistance to wage cuts. Edwards's restaurant analogy: a mom-and-pop place does not reprice by the hour, so on a busy night it simply produces more and on a quiet night less. Output is demand-driven. In the long run prices adjust and output returns to Y*.

PAE = C + IP + G + NXPlanned aggregate expenditure. "Planned" investment excludes unintended inventory changes.
C = C̄ + c·(Y − T)C̄ is autonomous consumption; c is the marginal propensity to consume, between 0 and 1; Y − T is disposable income.

Substituting gives the expenditure line:

PAE = (C̄ − cT + IP + G + NX) + cYIntercept = autonomous spending. Slope = the mpc. That is all you need to draw it.

The 45° line is where Y = PAE. Equilibrium is the crossing.

The multiplier, derived

Y = (C̄ − cT + IP + G + NX) + cY
Y(1 − c) = autonomous spending
Y = autonomous spending / (1 − c)

So a $1 rise in autonomous spending raises Y by 1/(1 − c). The intuition is a chain: round one, spending rises by 1, so income rises by 1. Round two, that income raises consumption by c. Round three, by c². Summing 1 + c + c² + … gives 1/(1 − c).

With c = 1/3, the multiplier is 1/(1 − 1/3) = 1.5. With c = 0.8 it is 5. This is why the change in GDP always exceeds the change in PAE.

A detail worth knowing: the macro mpc is an average across households, and poorer households have mpc near 1 while rich ones are closer to 0 — which is why the composition of a stimulus matters, not just its size.

What shifts the PAE line

ComponentMoved by
CReal interest rate r (↑r ⇒ ↓C); expectations about future income (consumer confidence); wealth (stock market, house prices); disposable income
IPReal interest rate r (↑r ⇒ ↓I, via present value from Unit 14); expectations about future demand — "animal spirits"
GPolitics, wars, natural disasters. Taken as given.
NXTaken as given for now; Unit 24 shows ↑r ⇒ ↓NX

All three of C, I and NX fall when r rises. That single fact is the entire transmission mechanism of monetary policy.

The Great Depression as a worked example

The October 1929 crash reduced wealth, crushed consumer confidence and created "wait and see" uncertainty. Christina Romer's data show consumer spending collapsed immediately — and specifically in durable goods like autos and furniture, not groceries. That is a fall in autonomous consumption C̄: the PAE line shifts down in parallel, and the multiplier turns it into a much larger fall in Y.

The moves
  1. Combine all components into PAE = (autonomous) + cY. Compute the intercept by setting Y = 0.
  2. Set PAE = Y and solve. Show the line "20 = (2/3)Y ⟹ Y = 30."
  3. For a shock, recompute the intercept, re-solve, and state both changes.
  4. Explain the gap between ΔPAE and ΔY with the multiplier 1/(1 − c). That sentence is worth points on its own.
Traps
  • Reporting ΔY = ΔPAE. They differ by the multiplier — that is the whole point.
  • Forgetting that a tax change enters as −cT, so its effect is scaled by the mpc.
  • Drawing PAE with slope 1. Its slope is the mpc, which is less than 1 — otherwise it never crosses the 45° line.
Drills · Unit 20
calcC = 10 + (1/3)Y, I = 6, G = 5, NX = −1, in trillions. Find the PAE intercept and short-run equilibrium Y.
Answer
PAE = 10 + (1/3)Y + 6 + 5 − 1 = 20 + (1/3)Y

At Y = 0, PAE = 20 — the vertical intercept.

Set PAE = Y:

20 + (1/3)Y = Y ⟹ 20 = (2/3)Y ⟹ Y = 30

Short-run equilibrium output is $30 trillion, which matches actual US GDP — the numbers were chosen that way.

calcNow C′ = 8 + (1/3)Y, I′ = 5, G = 5, NX′ = 0. Find the new intercept and Y. By how much did PAE change? By how much did GDP change? Why are they different?
Answer
PAE′ = 8 + (1/3)Y + 5 + 5 + 0 = 18 + (1/3)Y

Intercept 18. Setting PAE′ = Y: 18 = (2/3)Y, so Y = 27.

ΔPAE = −2 trillion. Autonomous consumption fell 2 and investment fell 1, while net exports rose 1, netting −2. It appears as a parallel vertical drop in the PAE line.

ΔY = −3 trillion. Bigger, because the PAE line slopes upward, so its intersection with the 45° line moves left by more than the vertical drop.

Why: the nonzero marginal propensity to consume creates a multiplier of 1/(1 − c) = 1/(1 − 1/3) = 1.5. And 2 × 1.5 = 3. Falling income drags consumption down further, which drags income down further.

calcCongress wants to offset that shock entirely by changing G. How big a change, and in which direction?
Answer

Increase G by $2 trillion.

The shock reduced PAE by $2 trillion. Government purchases enter autonomous spending one-for-one, so raising G by 2 restores the intercept to 20 and therefore restores Y to 30.

The key reasoning sentence: as long as the mpc is unchanged across the two shocks, the same multiplier applies to both, so an equal and opposite change in autonomous spending exactly offsets it. Match the shift, not the output gap.

Note the contrast with a tax cut: a $2 trillion tax cut would only raise PAE by c × 2 = $0.67 trillion, because taxes enter as −cT.

conceptWhy is output demand-determined in the short run but not the long run?
Answer

Nominal rigidity. Prices and wages do not adjust quickly or completely — limited information, long-term contracts, menu costs, customers who punish "unfair" price changes, workers who resist wage cuts. So when planned spending changes, firms respond by changing quantity rather than price.

Edwards's restaurant: a mom-and-pop place does not reprice by the hour, so on a busy night it just serves more and on a quiet night it sits idle. Over the long run prices do adjust, and output returns to potential Y* determined by labor, capital and technology.

This was Keynes's core contribution after the Great Depression: the short run needs a different analysis from the long run. From a classical viewpoint where prices clear markets, the Keynesian diagram looks absurd — which is why it was a genuine paradigm shift.

conceptDerive the multiplier and explain it as a chain of spending rounds.
Answer

From Y = (autonomous) + cY, collect terms: Y(1 − c) = autonomous, so Y = autonomous / (1 − c). A one-unit rise in autonomous spending raises Y by 1/(1 − c).

The rounds: spending rises by 1, so income rises by 1. That income raises consumption by c, so income rises by another c. That raises consumption by c², and so on. Total: 1 + c + c² + … = 1/(1 − c).

The economic content: my spending is your income. With c = 1/3 the multiplier is 1.5; with c = 0.8 it is 5.

conceptWhy did the 1929 crash cause a depression rather than just a bad year for investors?
Answer

Because it shifted autonomous consumption down hard, and the multiplier amplified it. Three channels: the crash destroyed wealth, it crushed consumer confidence, and the volatility produced "wait and see" postponement.

Christina Romer's evidence is the telling detail: spending collapsed immediately in durable goods — autos, furniture — the postponable purchases, while groceries held up. That collapse in demand led to a collapse in production.

On the diagram: a parallel downward shift of PAE, with Y falling by considerably more than the shift because of the multiplier. Unit 22 adds the second blow — monetary contraction driving the real interest rate up.
