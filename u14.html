Capital, Interest & Present Value

Class 14 · Chapter 21 Final
If you remember one thing

The price of any asset — bond, share, machine — is the present value of its expected future payments. So interest rate up ⇒ price down, and expected future payoffs down ⇒ price down. The final spent a whole question on this.

What "capital" means

Physical capital
Machines, buildings, trucks, computers, infrastructure. The capital in MRPK.
Financial capital
The funds used to purchase, rent or build it.
Investment
The flow of new capital into the capital stock. Also a component of GDP.
Human capital
Education and training. Counts as capital in the growth chapters.
On the exam
Fall 2025's keyed answer to "in economics, the word capital means" was all of the above — physical assets, financial assets, accumulated savings investable across borders. Do not pick the narrow definition there.

Formulas

MRPK = MPK × MRRent machines until the last one's marginal revenue product equals the rental price. Capital demand slopes down like labor demand.
PV = F / (1 + i)tOne payment F, t years out, at rate i. Capital is durable; time preference is why interest exists.
PV = F₁/(1+i) + F₂/(1+i)² + F₃/(1+i)³ + …A stream: the price of a bond; with Ft = MRPK(t), of a machine; with expected dividends, of a stock.
Buy until PV(MRPK stream) = purchase priceRaise i, PV falls, firms buy fewer machines, MRPK on the rest rises until equality returns. That traces investment demand. Same logic sank Silicon Valley Bank in 2023, holding long-term Treasuries into rising rates.

Stocks and bonds

Bond
The firm borrows, repays with interest. Price = PV of the fixed payment stream.
Stock
The firm sells a share of future profits, paid as dividends. Price = PV(expected dividends).
Two reasons prices move
Interest rates and expected dividends, for one firm or all. The pandemic is the clean experiment: Zoom's expected dividends jumped, Carnival Cruise's collapsed, prices followed.

Rules that earn points

  1. Write the PV formula before computing anything.
  2. For direction questions, ask which way the denominator or numerator moved. That is the whole answer.
  3. If i = 0, every denominator is 1 and PV is the sum of the payments. This exact question was on the final.
  4. For asset prices, write "price = PV of expected future payments," then name the input that moved.
Traps
  • Defining capital as money. It is physical productive assets — except on that one multiple choice, where the key is "all of the above."
  • Forgetting to raise (1+i) to the power t.
  • Saying rising rates make bonds more attractive so prices rise. Existing bonds have fixed payments; prices fall.
Unit 14 quiz4 MCQ · 2 FRQ · ~12 min
MCQ 12 pts

In economics, the word "capital" means:

  • AProductive physical assets like equipment and structures
  • BFinancial assets such as a bank account
  • CAccumulated savings investable across borders
  • DAll of the above
Answer

D — all of the above. That is the keyed answer on the Fall 2025 final.

The course uses the word in all three senses: physical capital in the production function, financial capital as the funds that buy it, and accumulated savings moving across borders.

A is the trap, and it is the sense you use everywhere else — it is the capital in MRPK = MPK × MR. On this multiple choice it is incomplete. Keep the distinction in written answers: physical capital is the stuff; financial capital is the funds used to purchase, rent or build it.

MCQ 22 pts

The present value of $100 received two years from now, at an interest rate of 3%, is closest to:

  • A$94.26
  • B$97.09
  • C$103.00
  • D$106.09
Answer

A — $94.26.

PV = 100/(1.03)² = 100/1.0609 = 94.26.

B is the trap: 100/1.03 = 97.09 discounts only one year. Forgetting the exponent t is the most common arithmetic slip in this unit. C and D compound forward instead of discounting back.

MCQ 32 pts

Firms come to expect that less labor will be available in future to work alongside their machines. The price of a unit of capital today:

  • ARises, because capital becomes scarcer relative to labor
  • BFalls, because expected future MRPK falls
  • CIs unchanged, since the interest rate has not moved
  • DRises, because firms substitute capital for labor
Answer

B — it falls.

A machine is worth the present value of its future MRPK = MR × MPK. Less labor alongside it lowers its future marginal product, shrinking every numerator in the sum. Machines are worth only what they will produce.

C is the trap: present value has two moving parts. The denominator did not move, but the numerators did.

MCQ 42 pts

In 2020 Zoom's stock price jumped while Carnival Cruise's collapsed. The best one-line explanation is:

  • AInterest rates fell for tech firms and rose for travel firms
  • BA stock price is the present value of expected future dividends, and the pandemic revised those expectations in opposite directions
  • CA stock price reflects the firm's past profits, which diverged in 2020
  • DCarnival's physical capital was destroyed
Answer

B — expected future dividends moved in opposite directions.

Stock prices move for exactly two reasons: the interest rate used to discount, or expected future dividends. The pandemic hit the second, firm by firm.

A is the trap. The discount rate is an economy-wide price; it cannot explain two firms moving in opposite directions on the same day.

FRQ 14 pts

A bond pays F₁, F₂ and F₃ over the next three years. (a) The interest rate rises. What happens to the bond's price, and why? (b) Write an expression for the price if i = 0.

Two or three sentences for (a). For (b) write the formula, do not describe it.
Model answer

(a) The price falls. The price is the present value of the payment stream, P = F₁/(1+i) + F₂/(1+i)² + F₃/(1+i)³. The payments Ft are fixed, so raising i increases every denominator and the whole right-hand side shrinks.

(b) If i = 0 then 1 + i = 1 and every denominator equals 1:

P = F₁ + F₂ + F₃
What earns the points
  • Writing the PV formula out before answering.
  • Saying the payments are fixed — that is why the price, not the payment, adjusts.
  • Naming the denominator as the thing that moved.
  • For (b), the sum with no discounting — not "the price is higher."
FRQ 26 pts

A machine will generate $1,000 of marginal revenue product at the end of each of the next three years and then be worthless. (a) Compute its present value at an interest rate of 3%. (b) Recompute at 8%. (c) Use the two answers to explain the slope of the investment demand curve.

Show the three discounted terms in each case. One or two sentences for (c).
Model answer
PV = 1000/1.03 + 1000/1.03² + 1000/1.03³ = 970.87 + 942.60 + 915.14 = $2,828.61
PV = 1000/1.08 + 1000/1.08² + 1000/1.08³ = 925.93 + 857.34 + 793.83 = $2,577.10

(c) A firm buys the machine only if its price is at or below the present value of its MRPK stream. Raising the rate from 3% to 8% cuts that value by about $250, so machines that were worth buying no longer are. Higher interest rate ⇒ less investment, which is why investment demand slopes down.

Note. The lecture slide gives the 3% total as $2,827.83, because it mistypes the first term as 970.09 instead of 970.87. The correct sum is $2,828.61. The difference never matters for credit — write the formula and your working, and either total will be accepted.

What earns the points
  • The formula written out with exponents 1, 2, 3 before any arithmetic.
  • Three separate discounted terms shown, not just a total.
  • A total below $3,000 and not far below — the sanity check for three years of modest discounting.
  • Connecting the fall in PV to fewer machines purchased, not just "investment falls."
  • Naming the rule: firms buy until PV(MRPK) = purchase price.
