Supply & Demand

Class 4 · Chapter 3 Midterm + Final

The workhorse. Everything after this is a variation.

Demand slopes down because marginal benefit falls. The demand curve is willingness to pay, and it is marginal utility. The first ice cream is fantastic, the second good, the third eaten on a dare, and by the sixth marginal utility has gone negative. Across a market, the same logic sorts buyers: the high-value users sit at the top of the curve, lower-value users further right.

Supply slopes up because marginal cost rises. The supply curve is the marginal cost curve. You already saw why in Unit 2: the low-opportunity-cost producer goes first (Bill, at 1 fish per coconut), and squeezing out more means turning to higher-cost producers (Chris, at 4 fish per coconut). In the world oil market it is Saudi Arabia first, US fracking later.

Both sides are assumed to be price takers — many small buyers, many small sellers, no collusion. That is the competitive model, and Unit 10 is what happens when you drop it.

Shifts versus movements — the distinction they test

The curves are drawn ceteris paribus — Latin for “other things being equal.” Everything except the good's own price is held fixed. That assumption is what makes the next distinction meaningful.

A change in the good's own price moves you along a curve. Everything else shifts the curve. Edwards asks this explicitly: would a change in the price of the good shift the supply curve? No — it causes a movement along it.

Shifts out (increase)Shifts in (decrease)
SupplyProductivity-raising innovation; cheaper inputs; more producers enterProductivity-reducing regulation; costlier inputs; producers exit; a tax
DemandNew complementary good; substitute's price rises; people get richer; becomes fashionableComplement's price rises; substitute's price falls; goes out of fashion or obsolete

Reading real events: which curve moved?

The past midterm's egg question is exactly this. Egg prices spiked in early 2023 and again in early 2025. Supply shock or demand shock? Supply. Wild birds migrate in fall and spring, spreading avian flu, and prices peak in January after the fall migration. A demand story would need bakers to suddenly want far more eggs in January 2023, not again until January 2025 — not credible. A supply shift also predicts price up and quantity down, which is what happened.

How you would actually estimate a demand curve

In real data you only observe the intersection, never the curves. The demand curve is revealed only when supply shifts while demand stays put. Working (1925) used weather-driven variation in Minneapolis potato production for exactly this. The modern version is randomized pricing: Dupas and Miguel offered preventive health products at randomly assigned prices and traced out demand directly, finding demand falls sharply with price and is small at market prices even for products with large health benefits — an argument for public provision rather than market allocation.

The moves
  1. Name the market explicitly: "the market for X," with axes P and Q.
  2. Decide which curve moves and in which direction, and say why in mechanism terms.
  3. Read the new equilibrium off the graph and state both P and Q.
  4. If both curves move, one of P or Q is ambiguous. Say which, and say why.
Traps
  • Shifting a curve when the good's own price changed. That is a movement along.
  • Shifting demand for a supply-side event. A tax on producers shifts supply, not demand.
  • Forgetting that "supply increases" draws as a shift right/down, which lowers price.
Drills · Unit 3
examWhich curve slopes downward, demand or supply? Why does it slope down?
Answer

Demand. It slopes down because the marginal benefit, marginal utility, or willingness to pay for a good falls as quantity increases. More is better, but at a decreasing rate. Across a market, the same thing shows up as high-value buyers being served before low-value ones.

examEgg prices spiked in early 2023 and early 2025. More plausible: a supply shock from avian flu carried by migrating birds, or a demand shock from baking? Explain.
Answer

The supply shock. Wild birds migrate in fall and spring, and the price of eggs peaks around January, right after the fall migration. Seasonal demand fluctuations exist, but there is no plausible story for why bakers would demand many more eggs in January 2023, then not again until January 2025.

The diagram check seals it: an inward supply shift raises P and lowers Q, which matches. An outward demand shift would have raised P and raised Q.

graphUC Berkeley opens Anchor House, adding 244 apartments for 772 students at market prices. Effect on price and quantity of rental apartments in Berkeley?
Answer

Supply of rental housing shifts out. Price falls, quantity rises.

Careful with the follow-up version of this question: if instead Berkeley grows its undergraduate class from 8,000 to 10,000, demand shifts out, so price rises and quantity rises. Same market, opposite curve, opposite price effect.

conceptEurope lifts its ban on fracking. Effect on the world price and quantity of oil?
Answer

World supply shifts out. Price falls, quantity rises. Note this is a genuine shift, not a movement along — the change came from technology and access, not from the price of oil itself.

conceptWhy can't you just estimate a demand curve from observed prices and quantities?
Answer

Because you only ever observe the intersection of supply and demand, not either curve. Prices and quantities move because both curves shift.

The demand curve is traced out only when supply shifts while demand holds still — as with weather-driven potato harvests in Working (1925) — or when the researcher randomizes prices directly, as Dupas and Miguel did with preventive health products.
